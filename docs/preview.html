Android home-screen widget  /  5 × 3 cells

Dot Matrix Player

Media controls for whatever is already playing — album art, transport keys and a scrub bar you can tap to seek — drawn in a monochrome dot-matrix idiom. The widget below is live, rendered from the same layout arithmetic the Kotlin uses. The shipping app draws its type with Ndot 77 JP Extended and Ndot 57 Aligned; this page approximates the dot face with a drawn 5 × 7 matrix.

Anatomy

Nothing's language is monochrome with one signal colour. Red is spent on exactly two things, and both of them mean “this is live right now”.

Album art
94 dp, square. Centre-cropped into the bitmap before it is parcelled — RemoteViews has no clipToOutline, so anything shaped has to be shaped at draw time. Left square: a sleeve is a square object, and the corners were the one soft edge in an otherwise hard-edged tile. On the two-cell layout it runs the full height at 86 dp.
Source app
Monochrome, 20 dp. Which app the audio is coming from. Adaptive icons can carry a monochrome layer — the one the system uses for themed icons — and that is taken and tinted when present; otherwise the icon is desaturated and clipped to a circle. Either way no third-party colour lands on the card. Hidden when there is no app to name. On this page it is an anonymous stand-in - there is no running app to read an icon from.
Source carousel
One dot per app holding a session. Several apps can own a session at once - a podcast paused mid-episode while music plays - and the tile has room for one. Tapping the source mark pages to the next; the dots say there is anywhere to page to, and which one is showing. Below two sources nothing is drawn at all, because a control that cannot do anything reads worse than no control.

A tap, not a swipe: RemoteViews supports no gesture beyond a click, so the mark and its dots are wrapped in one padded container to clear the 48 dp touch minimum without changing what is drawn. Never the platform's own recency order, which reshuffles underneath the widget and would make next land somewhere different each press — the ring order is arranged on the setup screen, and falls back to alphabetical until it is.

That settings list holds only apps the widget has actually seen holding a session, not every app publishing a MediaBrowserService — on a real device that is twenty-odd, most of them a newspaper or a cloud drive that will never appear in the ring at all.

A paged-to source outranks even a session that is playing — the user said which one they wanted. That pin expires after two minutes so paging once does not quietly break the tile for every song afterwards, and renews itself for as long as the pinned source is the one actually playing.
Empty artwork
The disc glyph, not a music note. Shown whenever there is nothing to draw album art from — no session, or a session whose app published neither an artwork bitmap nor a URI ArtworkTools could decode. Reuses the same disc-with-play- triangle mark as the source-app stand-in below, rather than a second invented glyph for the same idea: "audio, unidentified."
Status label
Ndot 57 Aligned, 10 sp. Drawn to a bitmap, not set on a TextView — see below. Reads NOW PLAYING, PAUSED, TAP TO RESUME, NOTHING PLAYING or TAP TO CONNECT.
Live dot
6 dp, #D71921. Visible only while audio is actually moving. Paused hides it and the label reads PAUSED.
Scrub rail
Dots, not a track. Played dots solid white, remaining at 20%. Rendered at the widget's true pixel width — a dot rail stretched by fitXY becomes an ellipse rail.
Playhead
The second red. A full-height bar over the rail. Turns white when paused, so the accent never appears on a stopped widget.
Transport
40 dp keys, 30 dp apart. Play/pause is the only one with a container: a 1.5 dp hairline ring. Skip keys dim to 27% when the session says it cannot honour them.

Four things that fought back

Each of these shaped the architecture rather than being worked around.

A widget cannot use a custom font at all

Two routes look like they should work, and neither does. Loading the face and applying a TypefaceSpan in code parcels only a family name, so the span arrives in the launcher meaning nothing. Declaring android:fontFamily in the layout looks safer — the launcher does inflate that layout with our resources — but AppWidgetHostView builds its inflation context with CONTEXT_RESTRICTED, and TextView only resolves a font resource when !context.isRestricted(). In a widget that test fails and the attribute is skipped.

Both failures are silent: no exception, nothing in the log, just the system face where the Nothing one should be. This page originally described the XML route as the answer; it was wrong, and the widget shipped in the default font until a phone showed otherwise.

Pixels are the only thing that survives the trip. TextRenderer loads the face in the app's own process, where it resolves normally, draws the string, and sends a bitmap. Every label is an ImageView. A Gradle check fails the build if a TextView reappears in a widget layout, because that regression is invisible until someone looks at a home screen.

~21,000 codepoints, because the text is not ours

Title and artist are the only two strings the widget does not author — they arrive from whichever app is playing, in whatever script it likes. Set in NType 82 they would cover about 240 codepoints: full ASCII, most of Latin-1, curly quotes. A Japanese or Russian title would fall through, character by character, to the system face.

Ndot 77 JP Extended covers ~21,000 — Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, kana and most common CJK — so it draws the title and artist instead. The trade is 14.9 MB of font, which compresses to about 1.4 MB in the APK.

The aligned cut exists for exactly this

Ndot ships in a proportional cut and an “Aligned” one. The difference is invisible until digits move: in plain Ndot 57 the numeral 1 is 40 units wide against 60 for every other digit.

On a clock that re-renders once a second, that makes the elapsed time visibly breathe every time a 1 enters or leaves. The Aligned cut is tabular — all ten digits at 60 — so the time codes are set in that. Its capitals are metrically identical to the proportional cut, which means one font resource covers the time codes and the status label both, and the other file need not ship at all.

Ndot 77 is proportional in exactly the same way, which is why it draws the title and artist but never the clock.

Album art hides in two different fields

Apps split roughly evenly between embedding the cover as a bitmap (METADATA_KEY_ALBUM_ART and its neighbours) and publishing only a reference (…_ART_URI). Reading just the bitmap keys is the obvious implementation, and it quietly shows the empty panel for half the apps on the device: Spotify embeds a bitmap, SoundCloud leaves every bitmap key null and hands over a content:// URI instead.

Both are tried, bitmaps first. Reading another app's content:// URI turns out to need no permission grant at all — Spotify's own provider served a 320 × 320 cover directly. Remote http(s) art is deliberately not fetched: the app holds no INTERNET permission, and keeping it that way is worth more than covers for the few apps that only offer a URL.

Two smaller hazards live in the same place. Art is decoded subsampled, so a 3000 px cover never lands in memory at full size just to be shrunk. And a HARDWARE-config bitmap — which apps do hand out — is copied to a software config first, because it has no readable pixels and would throw the moment anything tried to scale or shade it.

What happens after the app is gone

A media session vanishes the moment its app leaves recents, taking the metadata and the artwork with it. The honest reading of that is “nothing playing”, which is also useless — the thing you most likely want is the thing you were just listening to.

So every live frame is written to disk: title, artist, position, and the artwork re-encoded to WEBP. Writes are throttled to a track change or three seconds of movement, so a 1 Hz repaint is not a 1 Hz disk write. With nothing live but something cached, the tile shows that track, the rail dimmed where it was left, the skip keys dark — there is no session to skip within — and play rebound to RESUME.

Restarting a dead app is the part with no clean answer. It connects to the app's MediaBrowserService, which is what Android's own resumption tile does. Apps may refuse: several allowlist Android Auto and Wear and reject everyone else. A refusal is expected rather than exceptional, and the fallback is simply to open the app.

Scrubbing with no touch events

A RemoteViews tree receives no touch events of its own, so there is no drag to read an x-coordinate from. Instead, sixteen invisible tap targets lie edge to edge over the bar, each carrying the fraction of the track it covers.

Over a ~210 dp bar that is a ~13 dp target, and a four-minute song scrubs in 15-second steps. Each region aims at its own centre, so a tap lands where it looks like it should rather than at the region's leading edge.

Two layouts, and the second one turns ninety degrees

Both tiles carry identical view ids, so the renderer populates either without knowing which is on screen. But at two cells the tile is 110 dp tall — 94 dp of content — and the stacked arrangement simply does not fit. Forced into it, the artwork could only ever be as tall as one row of three: 42 dp.

So the two-cell layout runs the artwork down the full height on the left, with title, artist, scrub and transport in a column beside it. That puts it at 86 dp. The three-cell layout gained too — trimming the transport row and the scrub margins took it from 80 to 94.

Both used to be handed over together in a RemoteViews(Map<SizeF, …>) so the launcher could swap on resize with no round trip. That stopped being viable once every label became a bitmap: one variant measures ~460 KB, and two would sit against the 1 MB binder limit. Now one variant goes over at a time and a resize costs a repaint.

The catch that has not changed: a RemoteViews action aimed at a missing id throws at apply(). The compact layout therefore keeps the labels it has no room for as gone views rather than omitting them.

Grid arithmetic

Android sizes widget cells at 70n − 30 dp. The 5 × 3 minimum is 320 × 180 dp; with 12 dp padding the rows consume the remaining 296 × 156 exactly.

RowHeightBehaviour
Artwork + metadata 94 dp The only weighted row — absorbs extra height on roomier launchers. Art fills it: 94 dp square.
Scrub bar + time codes 12 dp + 8 Time codes are dot-matrix bitmaps; the bar takes whatever width is left between them.
Transport 36 dp + 6 Fixed. Three 36 dp keys, 30 dp gaps, centred on the tile's midline.
Total 156 dp Against 180 dp minus 24 dp of padding. targetCellWidth/Height pin it to 5 × 3 on Android 12+.

Pulled down to 5 × 2 the tile is 320 × 110 dp, leaving 94 dp after 8 dp of padding — the same number, but now it is the whole height rather than one row of three. That is the entire reason the layout turns sideways: 86 dp of artwork on the left, and a column holding title, artist, scrub and transport in 87 of the 94. The time codes do not fit and are not drawn.

What makes it repaint

Two triggers, and updatePeriodMillis is zero — the system never polls the app.

TriggerCostCovers
MediaController.Callback free Track changes, play/pause, and apps handing playback off to one another. Event-driven, instant.
1-second ticker gated Only so the scrub bar advances. Runs only while audio is playing, the screen is on, and a widget is actually placed. Any one going false stops it.

Why it needs notification access

The one permission prompt, and the reason for it.

MediaSessionManager.getActiveSessions() is the only way for a third-party app to read and control another app's playback, and it will only return sessions to a caller with an enabled NotificationListenerService. That is the sole reason the app asks for it. The service never reads a notification — it exists to satisfy that check and to host the refresh logic. There are no <uses-permission> entries in the manifest at all, and nothing leaves the device.
